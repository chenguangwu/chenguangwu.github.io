📐 Formula

Battery-side energy = battery capacity × (target SOC − start SOC) ÷ 100; grid energy = battery-side energy ÷ charging efficiency; charging cost = grid energy × tariff; charging time ≈ grid energy ÷ charger power; added range = battery-side energy ÷ consumption per 100 km × 100; cost per km = cost ÷ added range; saving per 100 km = petrol cost per 100 km − EV cost per 100 km.

⚡ Charging inputs

20%–80% is common for daily use; charge higher only before a long trip.

AC charging is commonly 85–92%; DC fast charging about 90–95%, lower in cold weather.

Home chargers are often 7 kW; public fast chargers 60–180 kW, limited by both charger and vehicle.

📌 Notes

  • Charging time assumes constant power; the real curve, battery temperature, BMS current limit and charger output all matter, and fast charging slows sharply near full.
  • Efficiency covers charger, onboard charger and battery losses; AC is commonly 85–92%, DC about 90–95%, lower in winter.
  • Battery-side energy is estimated from the SOC window and excludes battery aging, thermal management, climate control and sentry-mode loads.
  • The petrol comparison uses only fuel consumption and petrol price; maintenance, insurance, depreciation, plates and access rules are excluded, so treat it as an order-of-magnitude comparison.

📚 Deep Dive: Why Battery Capacity Alone Is Not Enough

A 60 kWh pack does not draw 48 kWh to go from 20% to 80%. Only 60 × 60% = 36 kWh enters the battery; the charger draws about 40 kWh from the grid at 90% efficiency. The bill is based on 40 kWh, not 36 or 60 kWh.

Charging time is not simply capacity divided by charger power. A 7 kW home charger drawing 40 kWh from the grid needs about 5.7 hours in theory; onboard charger limits, charger negotiation, battery temperature and tapering make it longer in practice.

Cost per kilometer is charging cost divided by added range. With the same 40 kWh, 15 kWh/100km adds about 240 km and 24 CNY means 0.10 CNY/km; at 20 kWh/100km it adds only 180 km, raising the cost to about 0.133 CNY/km.

❓ FAQ

How much energy does a 60 kWh battery need from 20% to 80%?
About 36 kWh enters the battery; at 90% efficiency the grid supplies about 40 kWh.
How long does a 7 kW home charger take?
For the 40 kWh grid-energy example, about 5.7 hours in theory; onboard charger and battery temperature can extend it.
How is cost per kilometer calculated?
Charging cost ÷ added range; added range = battery-side energy ÷ consumption per 100 km × 100.
How much does it save versus a petrol car?
In the example, the EV costs about 10 CNY per 100 km and the petrol car about 64 CNY, saving about 54 CNY per 100 km, or about 8,100 CNY a year at 15,000 km.
